\begin{abstract}
For language-model extraction pipelines that admit a fact only when more than one source supports it, extractor-realised corroboration, rather than the aggregation rule itself, was the dominant constraint in the evaluated settings. This paper measures how much corroboration public resources supply, how much extractors realise, and what counting distinct sources buys, for the conservative Noisy-OR of the DySECT system, which counts repeated observations as independent support by design. The resources supply corroboration that extractors do not deliver: half of DocRED's gold facts (50.2\%) have two or more evidence sentences, yet extractors corroborated at most 3.1\% of facts within documents (on BioRED; 0.98\% on DocRED), even with document-level extraction and a stronger model, and recovered few multi-document facts from two documents on a pre-registered 2,010-document redundancy subset. Aggregation lowered F1 in every configuration, within and across documents. Counting distinct sources instead of repeated observations raised the precision of admitted facts on real LLM extractions of synthetic products, and on DocRED and BioRED where extractors repeat themselves; in a pre-registered rerun the gain held with a stronger extractor, robustly on one dataset and borderline on the other, and with token-probability confidence, which was even more compressed than verbalized confidence. In DySECT's released knowledge base, 87.5\% of the concept generalisations its extractor can use pass its threshold only through repeated counts. Pre-registrations are verifiable from timestamped commits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models extract relational facts from text without
task-specific training, but their output is noisy. A common remedy is to
aggregate: store every extraction in a knowledge base, score each fact by
how much evidence supports it, and admit only facts that more than one
source supports. Such a rule can only work if three conditions hold. The
text must supply corroboration; the extractor must realise it, by
recovering the same fact from more than one source; and the repeated
observations the rule counts must be independent evidence
(Figure~\ref{fig:conditions}).

This paper tests all three for the conservative Noisy-OR with a
mutual-exclusivity penalty of the DySECT system
\citep{aminnaseri2026dysect}, which counts repeated observations of a
fact as independent support by design. The rule and the closed-loop
system around it are DySECT's and are attributed to it throughout
(Section~\ref{sec:related-work-dysect}); this paper evaluates an
independent implementation. That implementation's closed loop was also
evaluated, with null or negative results reported in the Supplementary
Material; the main text concerns the aggregation step. It asks four
questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{RQ1}] How much corroboration do widely used resources
supply to aggregation, within and across documents?
\item[\textbf{RQ2}] How much of it do language-model extractors realise,
and does corroboration-gated aggregation then improve on no aggregation?
\item[\textbf{RQ3}] Does counting distinct sources rather than repeated
observations improve what the rule admits, and does the gain hold with a
stronger extractor and with confidence derived from token probabilities?
\item[\textbf{RQ4}] Does the repeat counting that this correction
targets occur in DySECT's released system, and does it matter there?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The contributions are (i) a characterisation of how much
corroboration DocRED and BioRED supply
(Section~\ref{sec:results-availability}); (ii) a measurement of how much
of it extractors realise, within documents and on a pre-registered
cross-document subset
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-within}--\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}); (iii) a
distinction between observation multiplicity $O(t)$ and source
multiplicity $S(t)$ that applies to any rule aggregating repeated
extractions, not only DySECT's (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-properties}),
with a pre-registered test of the independence assumption on two
extractors and two confidence sources, whose precision gain also appears on real
DocRED and BioRED extractions where the extractor repeats a fact within a
sentence (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical});
and (iv) an audit of the rule in DySECT's released system
(Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}). Sections~\ref{sec:related-work}--\ref{sec:experimental-setup}
give related work, the rule, and the setup;
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:limitations} the results, their
interpretation, and the limitations.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Background}

\paragraph{Extraction.} Open extraction derives triples without
predefined relation types \citep{banko2007openie,liu2024openiesurvey};
DocRED \citep{yao2019docred} and TACRED \citep{zhang2017tacred}
standardised schema-guided evaluation. Generative
\citep{cabot2021rebel,josifoski2022genie,paolini2021tanl}, universal
\citep{lu2022uie,wang2023instructuie}, joint, open-world, and zero-shot
extractors \citep{wadden2019dygiepp,zhang2022oamine,levy2017zeroshot}
all treat extraction as a terminal step.

\paragraph{Knowledge and retrieval.} Retrieval-augmented generation,
including graph-based memories, uses retrieved evidence only at
inference time
\citep{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,asai2023selfrag,gutierrez2024hipporag,edge2025graphrag}.
Knowledge-graph population treats confidence as an open problem
\citep{bach2017psl,ji2021kgsurvey,nguyen2020kgcsurvey}, and attribution
is largely post hoc \citep{ribeiro2016lime,zhao2023explainability}.
Work on adapting extractors from their own outputs is reviewed in
Supplementary Section~S13.

\subsection{Closest Prior System: DySECT}
\label{sec:related-work-dysect}

DySECT \citep{aminnaseri2026dysect} populates a self-expanding knowledge
base with LLM-extracted triples, scores them with a probabilistic
confidence layer built on Theo \citep{mitchell1989theo}, and feeds the
knowledge base back into the extractor through prompting, few-shot
sampling, or fine-tuning. On DocRED it reports recall gains of 5--8
points for four models (Supplementary Section~S13). Its aggregation rule
is the one analysed here: its Equations 1--2 define
$C_{\mathrm{agg}}(t) = 1 - \prod_i (1-\lambda c_i)^{f_i}$ with
$\lambda=0.75$, followed by $C(t) = C_{\mathrm{agg}}(t)/(m(t)+1)$.
DySECT treats the frequency $f_i$ ``as repeated independent support'' by
design, with shrinkage as its guard against redundant evidence
\citep[Sect.~2.2]{aminnaseri2026dysect}; this paper tests that
independence assumption. DySECT's $m(t)$, ``the number of mutually
exclusive instances detected for $t$'', is a type-level conflict count
in its released code (Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}); this paper
keeps the functional form but counts rival values of a single-valued
slot, so the penalty's behaviour is a property of this instantiation.

% [aminnaseri2026dysect, arXiv:2603.06915v2, p. 3, Sect. 2.2 "Knowledge Base Growth", footnote 3, verified 2026-09-27]
The closed loop, its two feedback modes, and fine-tuning on
knowledge-base data are likewise DySECT's design, which the evaluated
implementation follows with minor differences (Supplementary
Section~S13); this paper claims none of them, and the two evaluations
are not directly comparable.

Not yet measured for this family of rules are how much corroboration the
text supplies and extractors realise, whether the independence
assumption holds, and how the rule behaves in the released system. The
main text measures these; the closed-loop evaluation of the
implementation (a thirty-seed ablation, a leakage-free fine-tuning test,
and calibration) found no end-to-end benefit and is in Supplementary
Sections~S15--S18.

\section{The Aggregation Rule}
\label{sec:formal-analysis}

\subsection{Definition}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-definition}

The rule is the aggregation step of a closed-loop system that follows
DySECT's design (Supplementary Section~S13). For a slot with candidate
object $t$ supported by observations with self-reported confidences
$c_1,\dots,c_n \in [0,1]$ and shrinkage $\lambda \in (0,1]$ (default
$\lambda=0.75$)
% [scripts/dec003_product_probkb_run.py; matches aminnaseri2026dysect Eq. 1]
, the \emph{conservative Noisy-OR support} is
\begin{equation}
A(t) \;=\; 1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(1 - \lambda c_i\bigr).
\label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
For a slot designated functionally single-valued, with $m(t)$ competing
object values already accepted for the same (subject, predicate) slot,
the admitted confidence is
\begin{equation}
C(t) \;=\; \frac{A(t)}{m(t) + 1}.
\label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The form is DySECT's; here $m(t)$ counts rival values rather than
DySECT's type-level conflicts (Section~\ref{sec:related-work-dysect}).
For a non-functional predicate (only \texttt{has\_color} in the product
domain), $C(t) = A(t)$. A candidate is admitted when $C(t) \ge \tau$,
with $\tau=0.88$ in the product domain
% [configs/functional_predicates_product_domain.json; scripts/dec003_product_probkb_run.py]
. Three properties matter here; the others, with proofs, are in
Supplementary Section~S14.

\subsection{Properties Used in This Paper}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-properties}

\begin{proposition}[Corroboration requirement]
\label{prop:corroboration}
With $\lambda=0.75$ and $\tau=0.88$, no single observation, regardless of
its confidence, is sufficient for admission: a triple requires at least
two corroborating observations to reach $\tau$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A single observation with the maximum possible confidence $c_1=1$ yields
$A(t) = \lambda = 0.75 < \tau = 0.88$ from Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} with
$n=1$. More generally, admission with $n$ observations each at $c=1$
requires $1-(1-\lambda)^n \ge \tau$, i.e.\ $n \ge \ln(1-\tau)/\ln(1-\lambda)
= \ln(0.12)/\ln(0.25) \approx 1.53$, so $n \ge 2$.
\end{proof}
For example, a fact supported only by one structured observation (fixed
confidence $0.98$) scores $A(t) = 0.735$, below $\tau$, however
trustworthy that source is.
% [EVID-041; src/extractors/structured.py]

\begin{proposition}[Rival ceiling]
\label{prop:ceiling}
For any functionally single-valued predicate slot with $m(t) \ge 1$
competing object values, $C(t) < 1/(m(t)+1) \le 0.5$, however much
support $A(t)$ accumulates; at any $\tau \ge 0.5$, such a contested slot
is never admitted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each factor $1-\lambda c_i$ is positive, so $A(t) < 1$ for any finite set
of observations, and $C(t) = A(t)/(m(t)+1) < 1/(m(t)+1) \le 1/2$ for
$m(t) \ge 1$.
\end{proof}
The ceiling is a designed property: it keeps a contested value out, and
a variant without it admitted exactly the same facts here
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}). Since accepted candidates
are never deleted (Supplementary Section~S13), a contested slot stays
unresolved however much evidence accumulates.

\begin{proposition}[Independence assumption and repeated observation]
\label{prop:independence}
Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} is a Noisy-OR combination rule, which assumes
each $c_i$ is drawn from a conditionally independent evidence source;
DySECT adopts this by design for repeated observations. The assumption
is violated whenever observations that share a source
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}) are counted separately. In particular,
when the same extractor, invoked at temperature $0$, re-reads the same
source text, the output is deterministic, so repeated invocations
produce identical observations that Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} treats as
independent corroboration, inflating $A(t)$ by the saturation property
(Supplementary Section~S14) whether or not the claim is correct.
\end{proposition}
In the 200-product snapshot, 31 unstructured-only triples were observed
2 to 21 times each by this mechanism, and all 31 were incorrect
% [EVID-029]
. On DocRED and BioRED the shared-source observations are of the other
kind, duplicate emissions of one fact from a single sentence
(Section~\ref{sec:results-within}).

Formally, let $O(t)$ be the number of observations of $t$ and $S(t)$ the
number of distinct sources among them (Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}).
Then $O(t) \ge S(t)$, with equality exactly when no source is observed
more than once. Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} multiplies over all $O(t)$
observations, which matches its independence assumption only if every
observation comes from its own source: it treats $O(t)$ as if it were
$S(t)$. The corrected rule R3 (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical})
evaluates Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} over the $S(t)$ distinct sources
instead, keeping each source's highest confidence, so the published rule
and R3 coincide exactly when $O(t) = S(t)$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:setup-datasets}

\paragraph{Public corpora.} \textbf{DocRED} \citep{yao2019docred}: the
845 documents of the 998-document human-annotated development split
(\texttt{thunlp/DocRED}) that contain at least one gold fact with two or
more evidence sentences, with 6,861 sentences and 11,344 entity-level
gold facts; the extractor is run on every sentence. These scores are not
comparable with published DocRED results, whose systems are trained on
DocRED and given the gold entities, whereas these extractors are
zero-shot and given no entity list; the official metrics, with the same
conclusions, are in Supplementary Section~S10.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-031, DEC-034; EVID-046; EVID-049]
\textbf{BioRED} \citep{luo2022biored}: the 500 Train (400) and Dev
(100) abstracts, with 5,462 sentences and 4,906 gold facts (unordered
concept pairs, eight relation types) and no evidence annotations.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-032; EVID-047]

\paragraph{Cross-document subset.} To test corroboration across
documents, a subset was built from DocRED's annotated development and
training documents: every document containing a gold fact that also
appears in another, a fact being identified by its relation and the
normalised first-mention names of its entities. It has 2,010 documents
(477 development, 1,533 training), 16,222 sentences, and 24,591 distinct
gold facts, 2,130 of them stated in two or more of its documents; each
document is one source. Results are reported separately for three
geographic relations (GEO: country; located in the administrative
territorial entity; contains administrative territorial entity) and all
others (OTHER), never pooled. The document list was committed before any
extraction.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-037, DEC-038; EVID-053; outputs/dec038_crossdoc/doc_list.json]

\paragraph{Product snapshots.} The rule comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}) replays real LLM
extractions of synthetic products from a deterministic generator
(Supplementary Section~S1). Synthetic products give the comparison full
ground-truth control: which facts each product's record and text state,
and therefore which repeated or conflicting observations are correct, is
known exactly, which no real corpus provides. \textbf{The structured records and the gold
triples come from the same generator call}
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Two knowledge bases are replayed, built
over the training products of a 50-product run (dataset A: 657
candidate triples) and a 200-product run (dataset B: 2,241).

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:setup-models}

The pipeline's extractor is \texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct}
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, via OpenRouter at temperature $0$.
\texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v3.2} \citep{deepseekai2025deepseekv32},
given the same prompts and inputs, re-extracts BioRED, the protocol
check, the product snapshots, and the cross-document subset, so that no
conclusion rests on the 8B extractor alone.
% [EVID-013; Decision log.md, DEC-032, DEC-033, DEC-036, DEC-038]
On CaRB \citep{bhardwaj2019carb} (official scorer, 548 sentences),
Llama-3.1-8B scored $F_1=0.467$ against $0.571$ for DeepSeek-V3.2, and it
also scored below GPT-4o \citep{openai2024gpt4o}, Claude Sonnet 5
\citep{anthropic2026claudesonnet5}, and Gemini 2.5 Pro
\citep{comanici2025gemini25} on a 30-sentence subset (Supplementary
Section~S1).
% [EVID-032; EVID-035]

\subsection{Aggregation Settings and Confidence}
\label{sec:setup-hyperparameters}

The product domain uses $\lambda=0.75$ and $\tau=0.88$, DySECT's
defaults, over a four-iteration extraction loop (Supplementary
Section~S15). DocRED, BioRED, and the cross-document subset use
$\lambda=0.5$ and $\tau=0.70$, so that two corroborating observations
suffice (Supplementary Section~S1), and no conflict penalty, since no
functional predicates are defined for them; each document is processed
once, with one extraction call per sentence, or per document in the
protocol check.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-020, DEC-032]
Confidence is self-reported in response to the prompt, not derived from
token probabilities or calibrated; the product extractor asks for a
value between $0.80$ and $0.96$, and the public corpora use their own
prompts (Supplementary Section~S1).
% [EVID-021; EVID-041]

\paragraph{Token-logprob confidence.} The product comparison was
repeated with confidences from the token log-probabilities of the same
calls: the geometric-mean probability of the subject, predicate, and
object tokens (primary) and the joint probability of the object tokens
(secondary), aligned to the output by sequence matching so that both
confidences score identical observations. DeepSeek-V3.2 was routed to a
provider that returns complete log-probabilities.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-036; EVID-052; src/extractors/openrouter_llm.py item_logprob_confidences]

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:setup-metrics}

On DocRED and BioRED a predicted triple matches a gold fact when its
relation matches and its normalised subject and object equal a gold
mention of the head and tail entities (on BioRED, of either concept, in
either order); first-mention matching is secondary (Supplementary
Section~S7). The product comparison uses normalised exact-match set
comparison, and its recall, over the \texttt{gold\_unstructured} facts of
training products, is not comparable with recall elsewhere; CaRB uses its
official scorer (Supplementary Section~S1).
% [Decision log.md, DEC-031, DEC-032; EVID-041; EVID-046; EVID-047]

\paragraph{Sources and corroboration.} A \emph{source} is the unit of
text an observation comes from: a product's structured record or
description; on DocRED and BioRED, a sentence, or a cited sentence at
document level; on the cross-document subset, a document. Observations
share a source when the pipeline re-reads it (the product domain reads
each description in each of four iterations) or when the extractor emits
the same fact more than once, possibly spelled differently, from one unit
in one call (within-sentence duplicates). R3 counts each source once. A
gold fact is \emph{recovered from two or more sentences} when matching
extractions come from two distinct sentences, and \emph{corroborated}
when two of them genuinely support it (gold evidence sentences on DocRED,
co-mention sentences on BioRED; Supplementary Section~S11); the first
bounds the second.
% [src/dec033_protocol.py corroboration(); outputs/dec033_protocol/analysis_result.json g2_raw vs g2_verified]

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:setup-statistics}

Bootstrap intervals use 10,000 resamples and the percentile 95\%
interval over test-split subjects (rule comparison), documents (DocRED,
BioRED), or fact keys (cross-document subset, with a document-resampling
check); point estimates are observed differences. The DocRED, BioRED,
protocol-check, stronger-extractor, cross-document, and closed-loop
analyses were pre-registered; each pre-registration is a public commit
that precedes its first result (Supplementary Table~S19). The rule
comparison's primary analysis ($F_1$ at each rule's selected threshold)
was committed together with its first results, so its order cannot be
verified; its fixed-threshold comparison
(Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}) was added afterwards as a
secondary analysis, and R6 as an exploratory rule. Other intervals are
secondary or descriptive and labelled as such (Supplementary
Section~S12); nulls are reported as found, without re-running or adding
seeds.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-026 to DEC-038; README.md ground rule 5]

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{How Much Corroboration the Resources Supply}
\label{sec:results-availability}

The annotations bound how much corroboration aggregation can use,
independently of any extractor. Of DocRED's 11,344 gold facts, 5,691
(50.2\%) have two or more evidence sentences, but only 386 (3.4\%) name
both entities in two or more: most second evidence sentences refer to an
entity by a pronoun or other coreference, which single-sentence
extraction scored by entity name cannot match. In BioRED, 35.1\% of gold
facts have both concepts mentioned together in two or more sentences
(89.7\% in at least one). Across documents, corroboration is scarcer: of
45,212 distinct gold facts in DocRED's 4,051 annotated development and
training documents, 2,179 (4.8\%) recur in two or more documents, 65\% of
them geographic.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047; EVID-048; EVID-053]

\subsection{Realised Corroboration Within Documents}
\label{sec:results-within}

\subsubsection{DocRED and BioRED}
\label{sec:results-docred}
\label{sec:results-biored}

Extracting from all 6,861 DocRED sentences, aggregation lowered $F_1$
relative to no aggregation ($0.044$) under every matching key: the exact
key; the normalisation of Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}, which closed
about 0\% of the gap; and an oracle key mapping each string to its gold
entity, which closed 7.3\% of it ($+0.0031$, 95\% CI $[0.0017,\allowbreak 0.0047]$)
and stayed $0.039$ below no aggregation ($[-0.044,\allowbreak -0.034]$). Only 29 of
the 11,344 gold facts (0.26\%) were recovered from two or more sentences,
and 19 (0.17\%) were corroborated. On BioRED's 500 abstracts, aggregation
(R2) lowered $F_1$ by $0.041$ with Llama-3.1-8B ($[-0.049,\allowbreak -0.033]$) and
by $0.064$ with DeepSeek-V3.2 ($[-0.078,\allowbreak -0.052]$). The two extractors had
similar unaggregated recall ($0.096$ and $0.097$), but DeepSeek-V3.2 was
nearly twice as precise ($0.069$ against $0.036$); 0.75\% and 1.35\% of
gold facts were recovered from two or more sentences (corroborated: 0.73\%
and 1.35\%; difference $+0.0059$, $[0.0021,\allowbreak 0.0098]$). Many errors were
schema errors, not entity errors: Llama-3.1-8B produced 811 triples that
connect a correct gold concept pair with the wrong relation type, against
473 fully correct facts (Supplementary Section~S16). Distinct-source
counting on both corpora is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}; per-method counts, the
evidence-only setting, and whole-abstract extraction are in
Supplementary Section~S7.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047; EVID-048; Decision log.md, DEC-031, DEC-032]

\subsubsection{Document-Level and Stronger Extractors}
\label{sec:results-protocol}

Because both runs extracted from single sentences, mostly with an 8B
model, a pre-registered check crossed the two extractors with sentence-
and document-level extraction on both corpora, counting corroboration
only from cited sentences that genuinely support the fact
(Table~\ref{tab:protocol-results}; design in Supplementary
Section~S11). Document-level extraction raised corroboration
significantly: DeepSeek-V3.2 corroborated 0.98\% of DocRED's facts and
3.10\% of BioRED's, against 0.17\% and 0.73\% for the Llama-3.1-8B
sentence-level reference ($+0.0081$, $[0.0056,\allowbreak 0.0110]$; $+0.0236$,
$[0.0169,\allowbreak 0.0314]$). A stronger extractor at sentence level did not
($+0.0002$, $[-0.0011,\allowbreak 0.0015]$, DocRED). No configuration came near the
pre-registered threshold of a fifth of the annotation-level rate ($\rho
\ge 0.2$; highest $0.088$ on BioRED, $0.020$ on DocRED), and aggregation

\subsection{Counting Distinct Sources}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}

The rule comparison replays the stored observations of the two product
snapshots (dataset A: 657 triples from 35 products; B: 2,241 from 140)
through six rules applied to identical observations, among them R1,
max-merge; R2, the published rule; R3, which applies
Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} over distinct (source type, source identifier)
pairs, keeping each source's maximum confidence, and leaves
Equation~\ref{eq:conflict} unchanged; and R4 and R6, evidence-share
reformulations of Equation~\ref{eq:conflict} (all six in Supplementary
Section~S2). Each rule is evaluated at its own validation-selected
threshold (the pre-registered primary) and at $\tau=0.88$, on held-out
subjects (35 and 92), with bootstrap intervals over subjects.
% [EVID-041; EVID-013/014/036 (dataset A); EVID-029/030 (dataset B)]

\paragraph{The ceiling holds and does no harm.} R2 admitted no contested
single-valued slot on either dataset at either threshold, while max-merge
admitted 6 and 40 at its selected thresholds and 20 and 69 at
$\tau=0.88$, where its precision loss offset its recall gain
(Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}). R4, which removes the ceiling,
admitted exactly R2's set ($F_1$ difference CI $[0,0]$), since no
contested score came near the threshold (maximum $0.581$); R6 degenerates
to $C(t)=1$ for uncontested slots and was below R2 on dataset A ($F_1$
CI $[-0.087,\allowbreak -0.012]$) and not different on B (Supplementary
Section~S2).

\paragraph{Distinct-source counting (R3).} At each rule's own selected
threshold R3 raised $F_1$ ($+0.0101$, $[0.0028,\allowbreak 0.0222]$; $+0.0039$,
$[0.0013,\allowbreak 0.0075]$), and at $\tau=0.88$ it raised precision on both
datasets at exactly unchanged recall
(Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}).

\subsection{The Rule in DySECT's Released System}
\label{sec:results-dysect}

DySECT's released code (\texttt{megagonlabs/dysect}, commit
\texttt{8d6c680}) and the knowledge base shipped from its DocRED
demonstration run were audited. The code does not deduplicate by source:
every stored observation, including repeated extractions by the same
model from the same prompt and document, enters the Noisy-OR once per
occurrence, as the DySECT paper describes
\citep[Sect.~2.2]{aminnaseri2026dysect}; R3 tests this design choice.
It is active in the released run: 98.7\% of the 61,772 stored triples
carry exactly the repeat-counted score, and of the 4,604 concept
generalisations above the $0.5$ threshold at which DySECT's extractor
injects them into its DocRED prompt, 4,030 (87.5\%) exceed it only
because the same source or document was counted more than once. The
mutual-exclusion division never applied (0 of 61,676 stored scores);
DySECT's $m(t)$ counts type-level conflicts, not rival slot values. A
check that reran DySECT's knowledge-base-guided DocRED extraction with a
source-deduplicated knowledge base found no material effect on its
recall. DySECT's released data-preparation script also places 78 of the
100 documents used to build its knowledge base among its 500 evaluation
documents, a property of the released code, not a claim about the runs
its paper reports.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Aggregation Fails, Within and Across Documents}
\label{sec:discussion-docred}

A natural explanation for the DocRED result is exact-string matching,
but even the oracle key, which pooled 28 of the 29 facts recovered from
more than one sentence, closed only 7.3\% of the gap
(Section~\ref{sec:results-docred}). The failure comes from the scarcity
of realised corroboration: a rule that admits only corroborated facts
cannot admit facts that are never corroborated, whatever the key
(Proposition~\ref{prop:corroboration}). Part of the shortfall is the
protocol, since only 3.4\% of DocRED's facts name both entities in two
sentences, but document-level extraction and a stronger model moved it
only slightly, and where every target fact is stated in two documents,
extractors recovered at most 13.1\% of them even once
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-within}--\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}).
Corroboration that exists in the text is not corroboration the extractor
delivers. In the product domain the constraint is masked by the structured record,
which supplies the second observation the extractor does not
(Supplementary Section~S16). Whether a much larger or
coreference-resolving extractor would reach the rates a corroboration
gate needs remains open.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047; EVID-048; EVID-054]

\citet{aminnaseri2026dysect} report a recall gain from
knowledge-base-guided extraction on DocRED, the opposite direction; the
two results are not a controlled comparison. Their extractors are larger
than Llama-3.1-8B, and their recall is measured after
knowledge-base-guided prompting, not after a confidence-threshold
admission step. In their released system the repeat counting that R3
removes is active, yet a check found no material effect on recall
(Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}). One reading is that where scores
select prompt context rather than admit facts, the independence
violation changes the scores more than the outcome.

\subsection{Why Counting Distinct Sources Raises Precision Where Evidence-Share Does Not}
\label{sec:discussion-r3}

R3 is the one tested correction with a robust precision gain
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}); R4 is an exact null
and R6 is negative. An account consistent with all three is that they
target different parts of the rule. Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} counts
every repeated observation as independent evidence, by design, but
repeated readings of one source by one extractor at temperature 0 are
deterministic repeats (Proposition~\ref{prop:independence}). R3 corrects
this at the \emph{input} to the aggregation, by collapsing repeats; R4
and R6 rescale its already over-counted \emph{output}, changing how a
contested slot's confidence is divided but not how often a repeat
counts. The pre-registered rerun supports this account: the precision gain
persisted with a stronger extractor and with token-logprob confidence,
and the score stayed count-driven under both (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}).
The account was not tested against alternatives.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Synthetic products in the rule comparison.} The rule
comparison replays real LLM extractions of synthetic products whose
structured records, text, and gold sets come from one generator
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-datasets}). The domain names each fact
consistently across sources, is fully labelled, and has a fixed
13-predicate schema, which real product data would not; on real text,
aggregation lowered $F_1$. The product numbers are results under
favourable conditions, not estimates for deployment.

\paragraph{Confidence carries little information.} The extraction prompt
asks for a self-reported confidence between $0.80$ and $0.96$, and the
aggregated score is poorly calibrated (ECE $=0.333$; Supplementary
Section~S16)
% [EVID-036; EVID-041]
. Token-logprob confidence was even more compressed
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}). Neither gives the score
much information beyond counts; a calibrated confidence was not tested.

\paragraph{Model versions.} The API-hosted extractors are fixed by model
identifier alone, and the provider could change the model served behind
an identifier.

\paragraph{The scope of distinct-source counting.} R3 acts only when the
extractor emits the same fact more than once from a single source: on
BioRED it raised precision for Llama-3.1-8B (138 repeats) but not for
DeepSeek-V3.2 (6 repeats), and where the discarded repeats included
correct facts it cost recall. Its benefit depends on the extractor, and
it does not address the scarcity of corroboration across sources.
% [EVID-041; EVID-046; EVID-047]

\paragraph{Small counts and protocol choices.} R3's precision gains rest
on few admitted triples (on DocRED, 36 admitted and 9 correct), with
correspondingly wide intervals and no $F_1$ difference. The main
public-corpus runs extracted from single sentences without document
context; the document-level check used one untuned prompt per corpus and
ran DeepSeek-V3.2 at sentence level on DocRED on a random 400 of 845
documents; every run used one fixed threshold ($\tau=0.70$); and on
BioRED co-mention stands in for evidence annotations. At document level
Llama-3.1-8B returned unparsable output for 12.7\% of DocRED documents
(107 of 845) and 18.8\% of BioRED abstracts (94 of 500), against 0.1\%
and 0.6\% for DeepSeek-V3.2. These count as having no extractions, as
pre-registered, so those configurations understate that extractor;
excluding them gives $\rho = 0.010$ and $0.028$ (descriptive, not
pre-registered). The check's pre-registered rules overlap for
DeepSeek-V3.2 at document level on BioRED, which meets both the
``stands'' rule and an example given for a partial outcome; it is
reported as standing with partial relief.
% [EVID-046; EVID-048; Decision log.md, DEC-033]

\paragraph{The extractors.} Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct scored $F_1=0.467$ on
CaRB against $0.571$ for DeepSeek-V3.2 and was below all four external
systems on a 30-sentence subset (Section~\ref{sec:setup-models}). Every
main-text conclusion was also tested with DeepSeek-V3.2; the largest
available extractors, and extractors that resolve coreference first, were
not. The closed-loop results in the Supplementary Material were not
repeated with a stronger extractor.
% [EVID-032; EVID-035]

\paragraph{The cross-document subset.} The subset is built from DocRED's
own annotations. Recurrence is decided by normalised first-mention
names, so a fact whose entities are named differently in different
documents is not counted as recurring, and the documents are selected
because they share facts, not sampled naturally. OTHER relations
contribute only 758 multi-document facts, 561 of them in two documents,
and the precision gains rest on 13 to 71 admitted keys, whose intervals
widen under document resampling.
% [EVID-054]

\paragraph{The DySECT audit.} The audit is a static reading of one
released commit and the knowledge base shipped with it. The recall check
used a different extractor and judge from DySECT's evaluation and is
reported only by its outcome. Neither shows how DySECT behaves with other
data or settings.
% [EVID-050; EVID-051]

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Corroboration-gated aggregation needs text that supplies corroboration,
an extractor that realises it, and repeated observations that are
independent evidence. For DySECT's conservative Noisy-OR rule
\citep{aminnaseri2026dysect}, the resources supply some corroboration
within documents and little across them (RQ1); extractors realise almost
none of it, so aggregation lowered $F_1$ in every configuration (RQ2);
counting distinct sources raised precision where extractors repeat
themselves, with a stronger extractor and with token-logprob confidence
(RQ3); and in DySECT's released system repeat counting is a documented
design choice that decides whether most of its usable concept
generalisations pass the threshold, with no material effect on recall
(RQ4). The aggregation step cannot supply what the extractor does not.
Future work should test whether stronger or coreference-resolving
extractors substantially increase realised corroboration, whether
calibrated confidence changes aggregation behaviour, and whether
source-deduplicated scores alter DySECT's full evaluation.
