\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models extract relational facts from text without
task-specific training, but their output is noisy. A common remedy is to
aggregate: store every extraction in a knowledge base, score each fact by
how much evidence supports it, and admit only facts that more than one
source supports. Such a rule can only work if three conditions hold. The
text must supply corroboration; the extractor must realise it, by
recovering the same fact from more than one source; and the repeated
observations the rule counts must be independent evidence.

This paper tests all three for one published rule, the conservative
Noisy-OR with a mutual-exclusivity penalty of the DySECT system
\citep{aminnaseri2026dysect}, which counts repeated observations of a
fact as independent support by design. The rule, its default shrinkage,
and the closed-loop system around it are DySECT's and are attributed to
it throughout (Section~\ref{sec:related-work-dysect}); this paper
evaluates an independent implementation. The implementation's closed
loop (feedback, fine-tuning on knowledge-base-derived data) was also
evaluated, with null or negative results, and is reported in the
Supplementary Material; the main text concerns the aggregation step. It
asks four questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{RQ1}] How much corroboration do widely used resources
supply to aggregation, within and across documents?
\item[\textbf{RQ2}] How much of it do language-model extractors realise,
and does corroboration-gated aggregation then improve on no aggregation?
\item[\textbf{RQ3}] Does counting distinct sources rather than repeated
observations improve what the rule admits, and does the gain hold with a
stronger extractor and with confidence derived from token probabilities?
\item[\textbf{RQ4}] Does the repeat counting that this correction
targets occur in DySECT's released system, and does it matter there?
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The paper makes four contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] A characterisation of how much corroboration two widely used
resources supply. In DocRED, 50.2\% of gold facts have two or more
evidence sentences, but only 3.4\% name both entities in two or more; in
BioRED, 35.1\% are co-mentioned in two or more sentences; and across
DocRED's 4,051 annotated documents, 4.8\% of distinct facts recur in two
or more documents, 65\% of them geographic
(Sections~\ref{sec:setup-datasets} and~\ref{sec:results-availability}).
\item[(ii)] A measurement of how much extractors realise. Within
documents, extractors corroborated at most 3.1\% of gold facts, even with
document-level extraction and a stronger model in a pre-registered
check; on a pre-registered 2,010-document redundancy subset, they
recovered 0.8--4.1\% of multi-document facts from two documents,
reported separately for geographic and other relations. Aggregation
lowered $F_1$ relative to no aggregation in every configuration, within
and across documents: extractor recall, not the matching key, is the
binding constraint
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-within} and~\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}).
\item[(iii)] A test of the independence assumption. Counting distinct
sources (R3) raised the precision of admitted facts on real LLM
extractions of synthetic products. In a pre-registered rerun the gain
held with a stronger extractor, robustly on one dataset ($+0.229$) and
borderline on the other ($+0.008$), and with token-logprob confidence,
which was even more compressed than verbalized confidence. The
correction acts only where the extractor repeats a fact within a source
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}).
\item[(iv)] An audit of DySECT's released code and knowledge base.
Repeat counting is documented and active there: 87.5\% of the concept
generalisations its extractor can use pass its threshold only through
repeated counts. Its mutual-exclusion penalty never fired, and a check
found no material effect on its recall
(Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent The rule's properties are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis}, including a rival ceiling that keeps a
contested value of a single-valued predicate out at any threshold of
$0.5$ or more, a designed property. The pre-registered analyses are
verifiable from timestamped commits in the public repository
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-statistics}).
Section~\ref{sec:related-work} reviews related work and DySECT,
Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis} defines the rule,
Section~\ref{sec:experimental-setup} gives the setup,
Section~\ref{sec:results} the results in the order of the questions,
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} their interpretation, and
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} the limitations.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Background}

\paragraph{Extraction.} Open extraction derives triples without
predefined relation types \citep{banko2007openie,liu2024openiesurvey};
DocRED \citep{yao2019docred} and TACRED \citep{zhang2017tacred}
standardised schema-guided evaluation. Generative
\citep{cabot2021rebel,josifoski2022genie,paolini2021tanl}, universal
\citep{lu2022uie,wang2023instructuie}, joint, open-world, and zero-shot
extractors \citep{wadden2019dygiepp,zhang2022oamine,levy2017zeroshot}
all treat extraction as a terminal step.

\paragraph{Knowledge and retrieval.} Retrieval-augmented generation,
including graph-based memories, uses retrieved evidence only at
inference time
\citep{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,asai2023selfrag,gutierrez2024hipporag,edge2025graphrag}.
Knowledge-graph population treats confidence as an open problem
\citep{bach2017psl,ji2021kgsurvey,nguyen2020kgcsurvey}, and attribution
is largely post hoc \citep{ribeiro2016lime,zhao2023explainability}.

\paragraph{Adaptation.} Parameter-efficient, instruction, and preference
tuning adapt pretrained models from curated supervision
\citep{devlin2019bert,houlsby2019peft,lester2021prompttuning,hu2021lora,dettmers2023qlora,wei2022flan,ouyang2022instructgpt,rafailov2023dpo}.
Self-improvement methods generate their own supervision
\citep{madaan2023selfrefine,gulcehre2023rest,chen2024spin,wang2023selfinstruct,schick2023toolformer},
and continual learning stabilises sequential adaptation
\citep{wang2024continuallearning,li2017lwf}; none maintains a persistent
structured knowledge base as the source of supervision. AutoML
\citep{hutter2019automl,he2021automlsurvey,feurer2015autosklearn,feurer2022autosklearn2,erickson2020autogluon,das2020sagemaker,elsken2019nas}
treats data extraction as a fixed upstream step.


\subsection{Closest Prior System: DySECT}
\label{sec:related-work-dysect}

DySECT \citep{aminnaseri2026dysect} populates a self-expanding knowledge
base with LLM-extracted triples, scores them with a probabilistic
confidence and reasoning layer built on Theo \citep{mitchell1989theo},
and feeds the knowledge base back into the extractor through prompt
tuning, few-shot sampling, or fine-tuning on knowledge-base-derived
synthetic data. On DocRED it reports recall gains of 5--8 percentage
points at the first knowledge-base-guided iteration for all four models
it evaluates (GPT-4.1: 22.80\%$\to$30.62\%; GPT-4.1-mini:
12.72\%$\to$20.22\%; Kimi~K2.5: 32.03\%$\to$37.85\%; LLaMA-3.3~70B:
23.23\%$\to$28.78\%), and its knowledge base can be inspected and
curated by a user.

\paragraph{What this paper takes from DySECT.} The aggregation rule
analysed here is DySECT's: its Equations 1--2 define
$C_{\mathrm{agg}}(t) = 1 - \prod_i (1-\lambda c_i)^{f_i}$ with default
shrinkage $\lambda=0.75$, followed by the mutual-exclusivity penalty
$C(t) = C_{\mathrm{agg}}(t)/(m(t)+1)$. DySECT treats the frequency $f_i$
of an observation ``as repeated independent support'' by design, with
shrinkage as its guard against redundant evidence
\citep[Sect.~2.2]{aminnaseri2026dysect}; this paper tests that
independence assumption. DySECT's $m(t)$ is ``the number of mutually
exclusive instances detected for $t$'', which its released code computes
as a count of type-level mutual-exclusion conflicts
(Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}). This paper keeps the functional form
but counts rival object values of a designated single-valued slot, so
the penalty's behaviour and its ceiling
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis}) are properties of this
instantiation, not of DySECT's. DySECT also exempts fully trusted sources
(in its current version, human curators) from aggregation
\citep[Sect.~2.2, footnote~3]{aminnaseri2026dysect}; this paper analyses
the automatic path, which governs all LLM-sourced evidence in both
systems' evaluations.
% [aminnaseri2026dysect, arXiv:2603.06915v2, p. 3, Sect. 2.2 "Knowledge Base Growth", footnote 3, verified 2026-09-27]
The closed loop, the probabilistic confidence layer,
prompt-augmentation feedback, fine-tuning on knowledge-base-derived data,
and the names of DySECT's two feedback modes (Encouraging and
Prohibitive) are DySECT's design, and the implementation evaluated here
follows them (Supplementary Section~S13). This paper claims none of them.

\paragraph{Differences.} The evaluated implementation lacks DySECT's
KNN-based hierarchical concept abstraction and its curation interface,
and it seeds the knowledge base from structured records as well as text
(Supplementary Section~S13). The two evaluations use different
benchmarks, models, and metrics, so no direct numerical comparison is
made.

\subsection{Evaluation Gap}
\label{sec:gap}

Three things have not been measured for this family of rules: how much
corroboration the text supplies and how much of it extractors realise;
whether the independence assumption behind counting repeated
observations holds; and how the rule behaves in the released system.
This paper measures all three
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-availability}--\ref{sec:results-dysect}). The
closed-loop evaluation of the implementation, with a module ablation
over thirty seeds, a leakage-free fine-tuning test, and a calibration
measurement, found no end-to-end benefit and is reported in
Supplementary Sections~S15--S18.

\section{The Aggregation Rule}
\label{sec:formal-analysis}

The rule is the aggregation step of a closed-loop extraction system that
follows DySECT's design: dual-source extraction from structured records
and text, a probabilistic knowledge base, a feedback controller, and
fine-tuning on knowledge-base-derived data. The modules are described in
Supplementary Section~S13.

\subsection{Definition and Attribution}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-definition}

The aggregation rule is \citet{aminnaseri2026dysect}'s (their
Equations 1--2), with their default shrinkage
(Section~\ref{sec:related-work-dysect}); this paper cites it rather
than claiming it. For a slot with candidate object $t$ supported by
observations with self-reported confidences
$c_1,\dots,c_n \in [0,1]$ and shrinkage $\lambda \in (0,1]$ (default
$\lambda=0.75$)
% [scripts/dec003_product_probkb_run.py; matches aminnaseri2026dysect Eq. 1]
, the \emph{conservative Noisy-OR support} is
\begin{equation}
A(t) \;=\; 1 - \prod_{i=1}^{n} \bigl(1 - \lambda c_i\bigr).
\label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
For a slot designated functionally single-valued, with $m(t)$ competing
object values already accepted for the same (subject, predicate) slot,
the admitted confidence is
\begin{equation}
C(t) \;=\; \frac{A(t)}{m(t) + 1}.
\label{eq:conflict}
\end{equation}
The functional form is DySECT's, but DySECT's $m(t)$ counts type-level
mutual-exclusion conflicts, whereas here it counts rival values of a
designated single-valued slot (Section~\ref{sec:related-work-dysect}).
For a non-functional predicate (only \texttt{has\_color} in the product
domain), $C(t) = A(t)$. A candidate is admitted when $C(t) \ge \tau$,
with $\tau=0.88$ in the product domain
% [configs/functional_predicates_product_domain.json; scripts/dec003_product_probkb_run.py]
. The three properties below are the ones the argument uses; the rule's
other properties, with proofs, are in Supplementary Section~S14.

\subsection{Properties Used in This Paper}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-properties}

\begin{proposition}[Corroboration requirement]
\label{prop:corroboration}
With $\lambda=0.75$ and $\tau=0.88$, no single observation, regardless of
its confidence, is sufficient for admission: a triple requires at least
two corroborating observations to reach $\tau$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A single observation with the maximum possible confidence $c_1=1$ yields
$A(t) = \lambda = 0.75 < \tau = 0.88$ from Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} with
$n=1$. More generally, admission with $n$ observations each at $c=1$
requires $1-(1-\lambda)^n \ge \tau$, i.e.\ $n \ge \ln(1-\tau)/\ln(1-\lambda)
= \ln(0.12)/\ln(0.25) \approx 1.53$, so $n \ge 2$.
\end{proof}
For example, a fact supported only by one structured observation (fixed
confidence $0.98$) scores $A(t) = 0.735$, below $\tau$, however
trustworthy that source is.
% [EVID-041; src/extractors/structured.py]

\begin{proposition}[Rival ceiling]
\label{prop:ceiling}
For any functionally single-valued predicate slot with $m(t) \ge 1$
competing object values, $C(t) < 1/(m(t)+1) \le 0.5$, however much
support $A(t)$ accumulates; at any $\tau \ge 0.5$, such a contested slot
is never admitted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each factor $1-\lambda c_i$ is positive, so $A(t) < 1$ for any finite set
of observations, and $C(t) = A(t)/(m(t)+1) < 1/(m(t)+1) \le 1/2$ for
$m(t) \ge 1$.
\end{proof}
The ceiling is a designed property of Equation~\ref{eq:conflict}: it
keeps a contested value out of the knowledge base, and on the data here
it did no measurable harm, since a variant without it admitted exactly
the same facts (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}). Because the
implementation never deletes an accepted candidate (Supplementary
Section~S13), a contested slot stays unresolved however much evidence
accumulates.

\begin{proposition}[Independence assumption and repeated observation]
\label{prop:independence}
Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} is a Noisy-OR combination rule, which assumes
each $c_i$ is drawn from a conditionally independent evidence source;
DySECT adopts this by design for repeated observations.
This assumption is violated whenever observations that share a source
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}) are counted separately. In
particular, when the same extractor, invoked at temperature $0$, is
asked to re-read the same source text, the output is
deterministic, so repeated invocations produce byte-identical
observations that are treated by Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} as though they
were independent corroboration, inflating $A(t)$ by
the saturation property (Supplementary Section~S14) regardless of
whether the underlying claim is correct.
\end{proposition}
The violation is observable in the data: in the 200-product snapshot, 31
unstructured-only triples were observed 2 to 21 times each by this
mechanism, and all 31 were incorrect
% [EVID-029]
. On DocRED and BioRED the shared-source observations are of the other
kind, duplicate emissions of one fact from a single sentence
(Section~\ref{sec:results-docred}). The same rule's scores are poorly calibrated on real LLM extractions (expected
calibration error $0.333$; Supplementary Section~S16).
The computational cost of the implementation is analysed in
Supplementary Section~S3.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:setup-datasets}

\paragraph{Product snapshots.} The rule comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}) replays real LLM
extractions of synthetic products. Products come from a
deterministic synthetic consumer-product generator
(\texttt{src/datasets/product\_generator.py}, \texttt{random.seed(42)})
over a fixed 13-predicate schema (listed in Supplementary Section~S1).
For each product it produces (i) a structured record with all 13 facts,
(ii) a text paragraph verbalising a random 7 of the 13, and (iii) two
gold sets: \texttt{gold\_structured} (all 13 facts) and
\texttt{gold\_unstructured} (the facts verbalised in the text).
\textbf{The structured records and the gold triples come from the same
generator call} (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
% [src/datasets/product_generator.py]

Two sizes are used, both split 70/10/20 (train/validation/test) with
\texttt{random.Random(7)}, a seed distinct from the generation seed
(\texttt{src/leakage\_split.py}): 50 products (35/5/10;
\texttt{data/product\_split.csv}) and 200 products (140/20/40;
\texttt{data/product\_split\_200.csv}). The knowledge bases replayed
here (dataset A: 657 candidate triples; dataset B: 2,241) were built by
the implementation's four-iteration loop over the training products;
the leakage rules for the other splits are in Supplementary
Section~S15.

\paragraph{Public benchmarks.} \textbf{DocRED} \citep{yao2019docred}: the 845 documents of the
998-document human-annotated development split (\texttt{thunlp/DocRED})
that contain at least one gold fact supported by two or more evidence
sentences, with 6,861 sentences and 11,344 entity-level gold facts, of
which 5,691 (50.2\%) have two or more evidence sentences. Only 386
(3.4\%) name both entities in two or more sentences; most second
evidence sentences refer to an entity by a pronoun or other
coreference, which extraction from a single sentence, scored by entity
name, cannot match. The extractor is run on every sentence.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-031; EVID-046; EVID-048]
These DocRED scores are not comparable to published DocRED results:
those systems are trained on DocRED and given the gold entities,
whereas the extractors here are zero-shot, open, and given no entity
list, and the evaluation covers 845 of the 998 development documents.
Supplementary Section~S10 reports the official metrics ($F_1$, Ign
$F_1$, evidence $F_1$) for the same extractions, with the caveats of
that conversion; the conclusions are unchanged.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-034; EVID-049]
\textbf{BioRED} \citep{luo2022biored}: all 500 abstracts of the Train
(400) and Dev (100) splits (PubTator format); the Test split is not
used. They contain 5,462 sentences (the title plus the abstract, split
by a fixed rule) and 4,906 gold facts, each an unordered pair of
annotated concepts with one of eight relation types. BioRED has no
evidence-sentence annotations; as a proxy, 35.1\% of gold facts have
both concepts mentioned together in two or more sentences, and 89.7\%
in at least one.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-032; EVID-047]
% [Earlier 15-document pilots superseded: Decision log.md, DEC-020; EVID-024; EVID-047; Supplementary S7]

\paragraph{Cross-document redundancy subset.} DocRED and BioRED annotate
facts within single documents, so they test corroboration across
sentences but not across documents. To test the latter, a subset was
built from DocRED's development and annotated training documents: every
document containing at least one gold fact that also appears in another
document, a fact being identified by its relation and the normalised
first-mention names of its two entities. Across those 4,051 documents,
2,179 of 45,212 distinct gold facts (4.8\%) recur in two or more
documents, 65\% of them in three geographic relations. The subset has
2,010 documents (477 development, 1,533 training), 16,222 sentences, and
24,591 distinct gold facts, 2,130 of them stated in two or more of its
documents. Because geographic facts dominate, every result on it is
reported separately for the three geographic relations (GEO: country;
located in the administrative territorial entity; contains
administrative territorial entity) and all others (OTHER); no pooled
figure supports a conclusion. Each document is one source. The document
list was fixed and committed before any extraction.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-037, DEC-038; EVID-053; outputs/dec038_crossdoc/doc_list.json]

\subsection{Models and Hardware}
\label{sec:setup-models}

The pipeline's extractor, used for every product-domain, DocRED, and
BioRED run, is \texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.1-8b-instruct}
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3} via OpenRouter at temperature $0$.
% [EVID-013; src/extractors/openrouter_llm.py]
On BioRED it is compared with \texttt{deepseek/deepseek-v3.2}
\citep{deepseekai2025deepseekv32} as an external baseline, given the
same prompts and inputs. DeepSeek-V3.2 also re-extracts the two
product snapshots (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}) and the
cross-document subset, so that no aggregation conclusion rests on the
8B extractor alone.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-032, DEC-036, DEC-038]
As a check on extractor quality, on 548 CaRB \citep{bhardwaj2019carb}
test sentences under the official scorer, Llama-3.1-8B scored
$F_1=0.467$ against $0.571$ for DeepSeek-V3.2; on a 30-sentence subset it
also scored below \texttt{openai/gpt-4o} \citep{openai2024gpt4o},
\texttt{anthropic/claude-sonnet-5} \citep{anthropic2026claudesonnet5},
and \texttt{google/gemini-2.5-pro} \citep{comanici2025gemini25}
(Supplementary Section~S1).
% [EVID-032; EVID-035]

\subsection{Hyperparameters and Prompts}
\label{sec:setup-hyperparameters}

The product domain uses shrinkage $\lambda=0.75$ and admission threshold
$\tau=0.88$, the default \citet{aminnaseri2026dysect} report.
% [scripts/dec003_product_probkb_run.py; scripts/dec006_scaleup_probkb_run.py]
DocRED and BioRED use $\lambda=0.5$ and $\tau=0.70$, so that two
corroborating observations suffice for admission (Supplementary
Section~S1), and no conflict penalty, because no functional-predicate
policy is defined for them
(\texttt{configs/docred\_functional\_predicates.json} is empty).
% [Decision log.md, DEC-020, DEC-032]
The product-domain loop runs four extraction iterations on training
products: structured facts seed the knowledge base at iteration 1 only,
and the LLM re-extracts at every iteration, guided from iteration 2 by
the feedback hint. Validation and test products receive one
text-only extraction pass, guided only by the trained knowledge base's
locked context.
% [EVID-013]
DocRED and BioRED documents are processed once, with one extraction call
per sentence (per document in Section~\ref{sec:results-protocol}); the
same fact extracted from several sentences supplies the repeated
observations.

Confidence is self-reported by the model in response to a prompt
instruction, not derived from token probabilities or calibrated
% [EVID-041]
. The pipeline's extraction prompt asks for a confidence between $0.80$
and $0.96$. CaRB, DocRED, and BioRED use their
own prompts, open-phrase for CaRB and enumerating the closed relation
sets for the others (Supplementary Section~S1).
% [EVID-021; Decision log.md, DEC-020, DEC-032]

\paragraph{Token-logprob confidence.} Because a prompted range of
$[0.80, 0.96]$ leaves confidence little room to vary, the product-snapshot
comparison was repeated with confidences derived from the token
log-probabilities of the same extraction calls: the geometric-mean token
probability over the tokens of the subject, predicate, and object values
(primary), and the joint probability of the object-value tokens
(secondary). Tokens are aligned to the output text by sequence matching,
so both confidences score identical observations. DeepSeek-V3.2 was
routed to a single provider that returns complete log-probabilities.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-036; EVID-052; src/extractors/openrouter_llm.py item_logprob_confidences]

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:setup-metrics}

Unless stated otherwise, triples are scored by normalised exact-match
set comparison (\texttt{src/evaluator.py}: lower-cased and
whitespace-normalised subject, predicate, and object, intersected as
sets). \textbf{CaRB} is reported under the official scorer (lenient span
matching; AUC and an optimal precision/recall/$F_1$ point), which this
paper cites; the internal exact-match evaluator is known to undercount
matches the official scorer accepts.
% [EVID-031; EVID-035]
\textbf{DocRED} is scored per document against gold entities rather than
strings: a predicted triple matches a gold fact when its relation name
matches and its subject and object, after string normalisation (Unicode
NFKC, case folding, punctuation and possessives removed, one leading
article dropped), equal the normalised form of any gold mention of the
head and tail entities. Predictions are deduplicated per
gold fact, and the same evaluator is used for every aggregation arm.
\textbf{BioRED} is scored the same way against gold concepts, matching
either argument order. For both, exact matching against each entity's
first mention (and, for BioRED, a relaxed containment match) is a
secondary measure (Supplementary Section~S7).
% [Decision log.md, DEC-031, DEC-032; EVID-046; EVID-047]

\paragraph{Recall denominators.} Recall is $TP/(TP+FN)$, and its gold
set is named wherever it appears. The rule comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}) uses the
\texttt{gold\_unstructured} facts of training products; recall in the
closed-loop experiments uses other gold sets and is not comparable
(Supplementary Section~S1).
% [EVID-041]

\paragraph{Sources and corroboration.} A \emph{source} is the unit of
text an observation was extracted from, identified by its (source type,
source identifier) pair: in the product domain, one product's structured
record or its unstructured description; on DocRED and BioRED, one
sentence (sentence-level extraction) or one sentence the extractor cites
(document-level extraction). Observations share a source when they come
from the same unit, by either of two mechanisms: the pipeline re-reads
the same text (the product domain reads each description once per
iteration, for four iterations), or the extractor emits the same fact
more than once, possibly in different spellings, from one unit in a
single call (the within-sentence duplicates on DocRED and BioRED). R3
counts each source once. A gold fact is \emph{recovered from two or more
sentences} when matching extractions come from at least two distinct
sentences, and \emph{corroborated} when at least two of those sentences
genuinely support it: gold evidence sentences on DocRED, sentences
mentioning both concepts on BioRED (Supplementary Section~S11). The
first share bounds the second from above; both are given where they
differ.
% [src/dec033_protocol.py corroboration(); outputs/dec033_protocol/analysis_result.json g2_raw vs g2_verified]

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:setup-statistics}

\paragraph{Bootstrap confidence intervals.} All bootstrap intervals use
10,000 resamples and the percentile 95\% interval, resampling the unit
of analysis with replacement and recomputing precision, recall, and
$F_1$ from summed counts: test-split subjects for the rule comparison,
documents for DocRED and BioRED, and fact keys for the cross-document
subset, where a document-resampling check is reported alongside.
% [EVID-041; scripts/dec026_aggregation_rules_replay.py; Decision log.md, DEC-027, DEC-031, DEC-032, DEC-033]

\paragraph{Pre-registration; primary and secondary analyses.} The
DocRED and BioRED runs, the protocol check, the stronger-extractor and
token-logprob rerun, the cross-document analysis, and the closed-loop
experiments in the Supplementary Material were pre-registered, with
primary metrics and decision rules fixed before any result was seen.
Each pre-registration is a commit in the public repository whose hash
and timestamp precede the commit of its first result; for the DocRED
and BioRED runs the extraction logs also record a start time after the
pre-registration commit (Supplementary Table~S19). The offline rule
comparison specified $F_1$ at each rule's own selected threshold as its
primary analysis in advance, but that specification was committed
together with its first results, so its order cannot be verified from
the repository; its fixed-threshold
comparison (Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}) was added as a
secondary analysis after the first results, and one rule (R6) as an
exploratory check. Each experiment has one or two primary analyses;
every other interval is secondary or descriptive and is labelled as such
where it is reported. Supplementary Section~S12 lists the status of
every analysis. Point estimates are observed differences; intervals are
bootstrap percentile intervals. A result with $p \ge 0.05$, or a bootstrap
CI including zero, is reported as a null exactly as found, without
re-running, adding seeds, or post-hoc adjustment.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-027, DEC-029, DEC-031, DEC-032, DEC-033; README.md ground rule 5]

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Results follow the four questions: what the resources supply
(Section~\ref{sec:results-availability}), what extractors realise within
and across documents
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-within} and~\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}), what
counting distinct sources buys
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}), and how the rule behaves
in DySECT's released system (Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}).
Interpretation is in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{How Much Corroboration the Resources Supply}
\label{sec:results-availability}

Independently of any extractor, the annotations bound how much
corroboration aggregation can use. In DocRED's 845 development documents,
50.2\% of the 11,344 gold facts have two or more evidence sentences, but
only 3.4\% name both entities in two or more; the other supporting
sentences refer to an entity by a pronoun or other coreference, which
extraction from single sentences, scored by entity name, cannot match.
In BioRED's 500 abstracts, which have no evidence annotations, 35.1\% of
gold facts have both concepts mentioned together in two or more
sentences. Across documents, corroboration is scarcer still: of 45,212
distinct gold facts in DocRED's 4,051 annotated development and training
documents, 2,179 (4.8\%) recur in two or more documents, and 65\% of
those are geographic. These rates are properties of the resources, not
of any method.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047; EVID-053]

\subsection{Realised Corroboration Within Documents}
\label{sec:results-within}

\subsubsection{DocRED}
\label{sec:results-docred}

On the 845 DocRED documents, extracting from every sentence (6,861
calls), PKB aggregation lowered $F_1$ relative to no aggregation under
every matching key tested (Table~\ref{tab:docred-results}): the PKB's
existing exact key (lower-cased, stripped); the deployable normalisation
of Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}; and a gold-alias key that maps each
string to the gold entity whose mention it matches, an oracle upper
bound rather than a deployable method. Normalisation admitted 7 more
items than the exact key, all false positives, and closed about 0\% of
the $F_1$ gap to no aggregation. The oracle key raised $F_1$ by $0.0031$
(95\% CI $[0.0017, 0.0047]$), closing 7.3\% of that gap, and remained
$0.039$ below no aggregation ($[-0.044, -0.034]$). Of the 11,344 gold
facts, only 29 (0.26\%) were recovered from two or more distinct
sentences, and 19 (0.17\%) were corroborated by two supporting
sentences, against 50.2\% with two or more evidence sentences and 3.4\%
that name both entities in two or more, approximately the ceiling for
sentence-level extraction scored by entity name. Only 49.2\% of the
18,104 extracted triples used one of DocRED's relation names.
% [EVID-046; EVID-048]

Counting distinct source sentences (R3) rather than every observation
(R2) raised the precision of admitted triples from $0.060$ to $0.250$

discarding 154 and 194 within-sentence duplicate observations

the exact key), R3 lost a few true positives (11 to 9), and the
pre-registered $F_1$ comparison did not exclude zero ($-0.0003$,
$[-0.0009, 0.00003]$); the precision comparison was a secondary
analysis. Extracting from the gold evidence sentences only gives the
same pattern (Supplementary Section~S7).

\subsubsection{Document-Level and Stronger Extractors}
\label{sec:results-protocol}

Both runs above extracted from single sentences, mostly with an 8B
model, so the scarce corroboration could be an artefact of that
protocol. A pre-registered check crossed the two extractors with two
units, sentence and whole document, on both corpora
(Table~\ref{tab:protocol-results}). At document level the extractor
cites the sentences that support each triple, and corroboration is
counted only from cited sentences that genuinely support the fact
(Supplementary Section~S11 gives the design). The pre-registered rule
would have judged the extractor-recall conclusion weakened if a new
configuration raised corroboration significantly, reached a fifth of
the annotation-level rate ($\rho \ge 0.2$), and narrowed the gap to no
aggregation significantly.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-033; EVID-048]

Document-level extraction raised corroboration significantly on both
corpora: DeepSeek-V3.2 corroborated 0.98\% of DocRED's gold facts and
3.10\% of BioRED's by two or more supporting sentences, against 0.17\%
and 0.73\% for the Llama-3.1-8B sentence-level reference (differences
$+0.0081$, 95\% CI $[0.0056, 0.0110]$, and $+0.0236$,
$[0.0169, 0.0314]$). A stronger extractor alone did not: at sentence
level on DocRED, DeepSeek-V3.2 doubled recall but left corroboration
unchanged ($+0.0002$, $[-0.0011, 0.0015]$). No configuration came near
the threshold: the highest $\rho$ was $0.088$ (BioRED) and $0.020$
(DocRED), and aggregation lowered $F_1$ relative to no aggregation in
all eight configurations, each with a confidence interval below zero.
The conclusion therefore stands by the pre-registered rule, with
partial relief on BioRED: there, DeepSeek-V3.2 at document level
narrowed the gap to no aggregation significantly ($+0.016$,
$[0.003, 0.029]$), from $-0.043$ to $-0.026$ ($[-0.038, -0.016]$), and
raised the recall of the aggregated output from $0.005$ to $0.039$. This
configuration also matches an example the pre-registration gave for a
partial outcome, so its rules overlap here; the stands rule, stated
first, is met (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). On DocRED the gap
widened, because DeepSeek-V3.2's unaggregated output was far more
accurate ($F_1$ $0.184$ at document level) while aggregation still
admitted almost nothing. Measured against the 3.4\% of DocRED facts that
name both entities in two or more sentences, DeepSeek-V3.2 at document
level reached 0.29 of that rate (descriptive). Llama-3.1-8B returned
unparsable output for many documents at document level (table note b;
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Realised Corroboration Across Documents}
\label{sec:results-crossdoc}

On the cross-document subset (Section~\ref{sec:setup-datasets}), every
target fact is stated in two or more documents by construction, yet the
extractors recovered only 3.6--13.1\% of these facts from even one
document, and 0.8--4.1\% from two or more (Table~\ref{tab:crossdoc}).
This is the same order as within single documents
(Section~\ref{sec:results-protocol}): extractor recall, not the
availability of corroboration, is the binding constraint across
documents too. Requiring two distinct documents lowered $F_1$ relative to
no aggregation in every cell, both strata and both extractors, each
confidence interval below zero. It raised the precision of the few facts
it admitted in three of the four cells (13 to 71 admitted keys), with no
detectable change for Llama-3.1-8B on OTHER relations, whose unaggregated
precision was only $0.038$. The strata did not behave alike:
DeepSeek-V3.2 realised less cross-document corroboration for GEO facts
than for OTHER facts, Llama-3.1-8B the reverse. In a secondary check that
resampled half of the documents, the $F_1$ decreases held, but the
precision intervals widened considerably, since they rest on few
admitted keys.

\subsection{Counting Distinct Sources}
\label{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}

Whether counting distinct sources improves what the rule admits, and
whether the rival ceiling (Proposition~\ref{prop:ceiling}) matters in
practice, were tested offline by replaying the stored observations of two independent
gold-labelled snapshots, 657 triples from 35 products (dataset A) and
2,241 triples from 140 products (dataset B), through six rules applied
to identical observations: R1, max-merge ($C(t)=\max_i c_i$, no conflict
handling); R2, the published rule; R3, distinct-source counting
(below); R4, an evidence-share reformulation
$C(t)=A(t)^2/\sum_j A(t_j)$ over the candidates $j$ of a slot; R5, R3 and
R4 combined; and R6, the unsquared share $A(t)/\sum_j A(t_j)$. Each rule
is evaluated at its own validation-selected threshold and at the fixed
$\tau=0.88$, on held-out subjects (35 and 92), with 95\% confidence
intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples over subjects. The full
per-rule tables are in Supplementary Section~S2.
% [EVID-041; EVID-013/014/036 (dataset A); EVID-029/030 (dataset B)]

\paragraph{The ceiling is confirmed.} R2 admits no contested
single-valued slot on either dataset at either threshold. Max-merge,
evaluated identically, admits 6 and 40 at its selected thresholds and
20 and 69 at $\tau=0.88$. It is therefore the ceiling, not an absence of
contested slots, that accounts for R2's zero. Max-merge's higher $F_1$
at its own selected threshold does not survive at $\tau=0.88$, where its
significant precision loss offsets its significant recall gain
(Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}).
% [EVID-041]

\paragraph{Two corrections that do not help.} R4 removes the algebraic
ceiling and reduces correctly to $C(t)=A(t)$ when $m(t)=0$, but its
admitted set is identical to R2's on both datasets ($F_1$ difference
CI exactly $[0,0]$), because no observed contested-slot score comes
near the threshold (maximum $0.581$ against a selected $\tau=0.73$ on
dataset A). R6, tested afterwards as an exploratory check, degenerates
to $C(t)=1$ for every uncontested slot (495 of 495 such rows in dataset
A), and its $F_1$ is significantly below R2's on dataset A (95\% CI
$[-0.087, -0.012]$) and not significantly different on B. Any
share-based replacement for Equation~\ref{eq:conflict} must reduce to
$A(t)$ when $m(t)=0$ to avoid this degeneracy; R4 does and R6 does not.
Neither is claimed as a contribution.
% [EVID-041]

\paragraph{The corrected rule: distinct-source counting (R3).} R3
targets the independence violation of
Proposition~\ref{prop:independence} rather than the ceiling: it applies
Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} over distinct
$(\text{source\_type}, \text{source\_id})$ pairs, keeping each source's
maximum confidence, and leaves Equation~\ref{eq:conflict} unchanged. At
$\tau=0.88$ it raises precision significantly on both datasets at
exactly unchanged recall, and raises $F_1$ significantly
(Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}). It also raises $F_1$ at each
dataset's own selected threshold, the comparison pre-registered as
primary ($+0.0101$, 95\% CI $[0.0028,0.0222]$; $+0.0039$,
$[0.0013,0.0075]$), so the gain does not depend on threshold
selection. Its effect outside the product domain is reported in
Sections~\ref{sec:results-docred} and~\ref{sec:results-biored}.

\subsection{The Rule in DySECT's Released System}
\label{sec:results-dysect}

DySECT's released code (\texttt{megagonlabs/dysect}, commit
\texttt{8d6c680}) and the knowledge base it ships from its DocRED
demonstration run were audited to see whether the counting behaviour
tested above occurs there. The code does not deduplicate by source:
every stored observation, including repeated extractions by the same
model from the same prompt and document, enters the Noisy-OR once per
occurrence. This is the behaviour the DySECT paper describes, treating
frequencies ``as repeated independent support'' with shrinkage as the
guard against redundant evidence
\citep[Sect.~2.2]{aminnaseri2026dysect}. Repeat counting is therefore a
design choice, and the source-counted rule tested here tests its
independence assumption. It is active in the released run: 98.7\% of the
61,772 stored triples carry exactly the repeat-counted score, and of the
4,604 concept generalisations above the $0.5$ threshold at which
DySECT's extractor injects them into its DocRED prompt, 4,030 (87.5\%)
exceed it only because the same source or document was counted more than
once. The released run never applied the mutual-exclusion division (0
of 61,676 stored scores); DySECT's $m(t)$ counts type-level
mutual-exclusion conflicts, not rival values of a single-valued slot as
in this paper. A check that reran DySECT's knowledge-base-guided DocRED
extraction with a source-deduplicated knowledge base found no material
effect on its recall. DySECT's released data-preparation script also
places 78 of the 100 documents used to build its knowledge base among
its 500 evaluation documents; this is a property of the released code,
not a claim about the runs its paper reports.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-035, DEC-035b; EVID-050; EVID-051; scripts/dec035_dysect_kb_audit.py; scripts/dec035b_dysect_stepA.py; outputs/dec035/]

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Aggregation Fails, Within and Across Documents}
\label{sec:discussion-docred}

A natural explanation for the DocRED result is exact-string matching:
the same fact phrased differently in two sentences would never be
pooled. The data do not support it: even the oracle key, which already pooled 28 of the 29
gold facts recovered from more than one sentence, closed only 7.3\% of
the gap (Section~\ref{sec:results-docred}).
% [EVID-046]

The failure comes instead from the scarcity of cross-sentence
corroboration in what the extractor produces: the sentence-level
extractor recovered only 645 of 11,344 DocRED facts at all and 29 from
two or more sentences. Part of that shortfall belongs to the protocol
rather than the extractor: only 3.4\% of DocRED's facts name both
entities in two or more sentences, so extraction from single sentences,
scored by entity name, could never have approached 50.2\%. A rule that
admits a fact only when it is corroborated cannot admit facts that are
never corroborated, whatever the key
(Proposition~\ref{prop:corroboration}). The binding constraint is
therefore extractor recall, compounded across sentences. The same
pattern holds on BioRED, with different text, a different schema, and an
external baseline (Section~\ref{sec:results-biored}). There the stronger
extractor recovered significantly more corroborated facts, but far too
few, and it lost more $F_1$ to aggregation only because its
unaggregated output was more precise while both aggregated outputs ended
near the same low $F_1$. The constraint binds in the product domain
too, but is masked there: every one of the 99 admitted correct facts
pairs a structured observation with an LLM one, while the 141
text-stated facts the LLM never extracted stay below the threshold
(Supplementary Section~S16). The structured record supplies the
second observation that the extractor does not. The aggregation stage
therefore adds value only
when the extractor recovers the same fact from more than one source often
enough.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047]

The pre-registered protocol check tested whether a document-level or
stronger extractor moves this boundary (Section~\ref{sec:results-protocol}).
It moves it, but not far. Extracting from the whole document, with
cited evidence sentences, raised corroboration significantly on both
corpora; a stronger model extracting from single sentences did not
raise it on DocRED. On BioRED, the best configuration narrowed the gap
to no aggregation significantly, the one partial relief the check
found, but it still corroborated under a tenth of the annotated facts,
and aggregation still lowered $F_1$ on both corpora in every
configuration. By the pre-registered rule, the extractor-recall
conclusion stands for the extractors and protocols tested. What the
check does not settle is whether a much larger extractor, or one that
resolves coreference before extraction, would reach the corroboration
rates that a corroboration-gated rule needs.
% [Decision log.md, DEC-033; EVID-048]

The cross-document subset removes a further objection, that single
documents rarely restate a fact. There every target fact is stated in
at least two documents, yet the extractors recovered at most 13.1\% of
them from even one document, and aggregation lowered $F_1$ in every cell
(Section~\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}). Corroboration that exists in the
text is not corroboration the extractor delivers.
% [EVID-054]

\citet{aminnaseri2026dysect} report a recall \emph{improvement} from
knowledge-base-guided extraction on DocRED for all four models they
evaluate. The two results are not a controlled comparison, and two
differences, neither tested here, may explain the opposite direction.
First, their extractors (GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1-mini, LLaMA-3.3~70B, and
Kimi~K2.5) are all larger than the Llama-3.1-8B used here, and the
finding above suggests extractor recall matters most. Second, their
recall is extraction recall under knowledge-base-guided
\emph{prompting}, whereas this paper measures recall after a
confidence-threshold \emph{admission} step, so the two figures may not
measure the same quantity. In DySECT's own released system, the repeat
counting that R3 removes is active, yet a check found no material effect
on its recall (Section~\ref{sec:results-dysect}). One reading is that
where scores select prompt context rather than admit facts, the
independence violation changes the scores more than the outcome.

\subsection{Why Counting Distinct Sources Helps Where Evidence-Share Does Not}
\label{sec:discussion-r3}

R3 is the one tested correction with a robust gain over R2 at the fixed
threshold (Table~\ref{tab:dec026-bootstrap-tau088}); R4 is an exact null
and R6 is negative (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}). An
account consistent with all three is that they target different parts of
the rule. Equation~\ref{eq:noisyor} treats every repeated observation as
independent evidence, by design, but repeated readings of the same source by one
extractor at temperature 0 are deterministic repeats
(Proposition~\ref{prop:independence}). R3 corrects this at the
\emph{input} to the aggregation, by collapsing repeats from the same
source. R4 and R6 rescale the \emph{output} of the already over-counted
$A(t)$, changing how a contested slot's confidence is divided but not
how often a repeat is counted. R6 shows that rescaling the output can
also hurt: because $A(t)/A(t)=1$ for any uncontested slot, it collapses
to a constant exactly where the input carries information. This account
fits the measured pattern but was not tested against alternatives. The
pre-registered rerun with a stronger extractor and with token-logprob
confidence supports it: the gain persisted under both, and the score
stayed count-driven under both (Table~\ref{tab:dec036}).

The same repeat counting appears outside the product domain, and it
defines R3's scope: R3 raised precision on DocRED (154 within-sentence repeats) and
for Llama-3.1-8B on BioRED (138), and had no measurable effect for
DeepSeek-V3.2, which repeated itself 6 times
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-docred} and~\ref{sec:results-biored}). Where
the discarded repeats include correct facts, R3 can cost recall. R3 is
therefore a correction for extractors that repeat themselves within a
source, not a general improvement to aggregation.
% [EVID-046; EVID-047]

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Synthetic products in the rule comparison.} The rule
comparison replays real LLM extractions, but of synthetic products whose
structured records, text, and gold sets come from one generator
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-datasets}). The domain names each fact
consistently across sources, is fully labelled, and has a fixed
13-predicate schema, which real product data would not. On real text
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-within} and~\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}),
aggregation lowered $F_1$ relative to no aggregation. The product
numbers should be read as results under favourable conditions, not as
estimates for deployment.

\paragraph{Confidence carries little information.} The extraction
prompt asks for a self-reported confidence between $0.80$ and $0.96$
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-hyperparameters}), and the aggregated score is
poorly calibrated (ECE $=0.333$; Supplementary Section~S16)
% [EVID-036; EVID-041]
. Token-logprob confidence, tested as the alternative, was even more
compressed (Section~\ref{sec:formal-analysis-empirical}). Neither source
gives the score much information beyond counts; a calibrated or
externally validated confidence was not tested.

\paragraph{Model versions.} The API-hosted extractors are fixed by model
identifier alone, and the provider could change the model served behind
an identifier.

\paragraph{The scope of the repeat-counting correction.} R3 corrects
the counting of repeated observations from one source, so it acts only
when the extractor emits the same fact more than once from a single
source. On BioRED it raised the precision of Llama-3.1-8B's admitted
triples by $0.054$ (138 repeats) but had no measurable effect for
DeepSeek-V3.2 (6 repeats). With Llama-3.1-8B its $F_1$ was slightly
lower than R2's, because some discarded repeats were correct
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-biored} and~\ref{sec:discussion-r3})
% [EVID-041; EVID-046; EVID-047]
. Its benefit depends on the extractor, and it does not address the
scarcity of corroboration across sources.

\paragraph{Small admitted counts on DocRED.} On DocRED, R3's precision
gain rests on very few triples: 36 admitted and 9 correct with the exact
key. Its confidence interval is correspondingly wide ($+0.190$, 95\% CI
$[0.084, 0.315]$), and its $F_1$ did not differ from R2's
(Section~\ref{sec:results-docred})
% [EVID-046]
. More generally, the main public-corpus runs used sentence-level
extraction without document context; the document-level check
(Section~\ref{sec:results-protocol}) used one untuned prompt per corpus
and ran DeepSeek-V3.2 at sentence level on DocRED on a random 400 of
845 documents. All runs used a single fixed admission threshold
($\tau=0.70$) and, on BioRED, co-mention as a proxy for
annotation-level corroboration.

\paragraph{Extraction failures and rule overlap in the protocol check.}
At document level, Llama-3.1-8B returned unparsable output for 12.7\%
of DocRED documents (107 of 845) and 18.8\% of BioRED abstracts (94 of
500), against 0.1\% and 0.6\% for DeepSeek-V3.2. These documents count
as having no extractions, as pre-registered, so the Llama-3.1-8B
document-level configurations understate what that extractor can do at
document level and are a weaker test of the protocol question than the
DeepSeek-V3.2 ones. Excluding the failures gives $\rho = 0.010$ and
$0.028$, still far below the pre-registered threshold of $0.2$; this
is a descriptive analysis, not pre-registered. Separately, the check's
pre-registered decision rules overlap for one configuration (DeepSeek-V3.2
at document level on BioRED), which meets both the ``stands'' rule and
an example given for a partial outcome; it is reported as standing with
partial relief (Section~\ref{sec:results-protocol})
% [EVID-048; Decision log.md, DEC-033]
.

\paragraph{The extractors.} The pipeline's extractor,
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, scored $F_1=0.467$ on CaRB against $0.571$ for
DeepSeek-V3.2, and was below all four external systems on a 30-sentence
subset (Section~\ref{sec:setup-models})
% [EVID-032; EVID-035]
. Every main-text conclusion was also tested with DeepSeek-V3.2: on
DocRED, BioRED, the cross-document subset, and the product snapshots.
The largest available extractors, and extractors that resolve
coreference first, were not tested. The closed-loop results in the
Supplementary Material were not repeated with a stronger extractor.

\paragraph{The cross-document subset.} The subset is built from
DocRED's own annotations. Recurrence is decided by normalised
first-mention names, so a fact whose entities are named differently in
different documents is not counted as recurring. The documents are
selected because they share facts, not sampled naturally. OTHER relations
contribute only 758 multi-document facts, 561 of them in two documents,
and the precision gains rest on 13 to 71 admitted keys, whose intervals
widen under document resampling (Section~\ref{sec:results-crossdoc}).
% [EVID-054]

\paragraph{The DySECT audit.} The audit is a static reading of one
released commit and of the knowledge base shipped with it. The recall
check used a different extractor and judge from DySECT's evaluation,
and is reported only by its outcome. Neither shows how DySECT behaves
with other data or settings.
% [EVID-050; EVID-051]


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Corroboration-gated aggregation needs three things: text that supplies
corroboration, an extractor that realises it, and repeated observations
that are independent evidence. For the conservative Noisy-OR rule of
DySECT \citep{aminnaseri2026dysect}, this paper measured all three. The
resources supply some corroboration within documents (50.2\% of DocRED's
facts have two evidence sentences, 3.4\% name both entities twice) and
little across them (4.8\% of DocRED's facts recur in two documents) (RQ1).
Extractors realise almost none of it: at most 3.1\% of facts within
documents and 0.8--4.1\% of multi-document facts across documents, so
aggregation lowered $F_1$ in every configuration (RQ2). Counting
distinct sources rather than repeated observations raised the precision
of admitted facts where extractors repeat themselves within a source,
and in a pre-registered rerun the gain held with a stronger extractor
and with token-logprob confidence, robustly on one dataset and
borderline on the other (RQ3). In DySECT's released system, repeat
counting is a documented design choice that decides which 87.5\% of
usable concept generalisations pass its threshold, yet a check found no
material effect on its recall (RQ4).

The practical conclusion is that the aggregation step cannot supply what
the extractor does not: until extractors recover the same fact from
several sources, a corroboration gate mainly discards recall. Each open
question could be settled by a specific experiment:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A much stronger or coreference-aware extractor.} Repeat
the protocol check and the cross-document analysis with the largest
available extractors, or with coreference resolved before extraction,
and test whether aggregation's gap to no aggregation closes as realised
corroboration rises.
\item \textbf{Calibrated confidence.} Replace self-reported and
token-logprob confidence with a calibrated one, and test whether the
score then carries information beyond counts.
\item \textbf{Source counting in a deployed loop.} Rerun DySECT's full
evaluation with source-deduplicated scores, with its own extractors and
judge.
\end{itemize}
